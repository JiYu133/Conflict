CONFLICT — REALISTIC MEDICAL AND INJURY SYSTEM STRUCTURE
=========================================================

DESIGN DIRECTION
----------------

Conflict is described in the README as a realistic tactical first-person shooter:

“在真实性与游戏性之间优先选择前者。”

This means the medical system should prioritize believable causes and consequences
over conventional game mechanics. It should not use a traditional health bar or
allow treatments to magically restore generic health. Survival should emerge from
anatomical injuries, bleeding, circulation, respiration, neurological condition,
and the medical care the player receives.


HIGH-LEVEL DAMAGE FLOW
----------------------

Projectile / explosion / fall
    -> Trauma event
    -> Anatomical damage
    -> Wounds, fractures, or organ trauma
    -> Bleeding, respiratory effects, pain, and neurological effects
    -> Reduced circulation or oxygenation
    -> Impairment, unconsciousness, incapacitation, or death

Medical treatment should intervene in this chain by managing the actual cause. A
bandage can control external bleeding, for example, but it cannot replace blood that
has already been lost or repair destroyed tissue.


CORE ARCHITECTURE
-----------------

The medical feature should be implemented as a componentized player subsystem,
consistent with the existing Conflict architecture.

Suggested files:

Classes/Medical/
    medical_system.gd
    medical_config.gd
    medical_state.gd
    medical_damage_context.gd
    medical_injury.gd
    medical_treatment.gd
    medical_body_region.gd
    medical_enums.gd

assets/config/medical/
    medical_config_default.tres
    injuries/
    treatments/

The MedicalSystem should own medical state and calculations. It should not directly
control animation, movement, ragdoll, weapons, or UI. It should emit signals, and the
other systems should react to those signals.


ANATOMICAL REGIONS
------------------

The initial anatomical model should support:

1. Head
2. Neck
3. Chest
4. Abdomen
5. Pelvis
6. Left and right upper arms
7. Left and right forearms
8. Left and right hands
9. Left and right thighs
10. Left and right lower legs
11. Left and right feet

Each region may contain one or more anatomical structures:

- Bone
- Major artery
- Ordinary blood vessels
- Muscle and soft tissue
- Organ group
- Nervous tissue

The first release does not need to simulate every individual organ. However, the
data format should allow more detailed organs and tissue structures to be added
without replacing the medical architecture.

Physical or animated bone names should be mapped to medical regions when an impact
occurs. Medical state should not be stored directly on PhysicalBone3D nodes because
ragdoll construction and model skeletons may change independently.


DAMAGE CONTEXT
--------------

All damage sources should create the same normalized MedicalDamageContext instead
of directly subtracting health.

Suggested information:

- Damage source
- Weapon or projectile
- Damage type
- Impact position
- Impact direction
- Impact energy or magnitude
- Hit bone
- Resolved anatomical region
- Penetration information, when available

Possible damage types:

- Ballistic
- Explosion
- Blunt trauma
- Fall
- Cutting
- Burn

The medical system should consume the resulting trauma event. Ballistic penetration,
armour interaction, and projectile energy transfer can later become their own system
without changing the medical API.


WOUND INSTANCES
---------------

Every injury should be represented by a persistent wound instance. Multiple wounds
must be allowed on the same body region; a second gunshot must not overwrite the
first one.

Initial wound categories:

- Penetrating wound
- Laceration
- Blunt trauma
- Fracture
- Burn
- Blast trauma

Each wound should track at least:

- Unique injury ID
- Body region
- Wound type
- Depth
- Severity
- External bleeding rate
- Internal bleeding rate
- Pain contribution
- Mobility or handling impairment
- Contamination, if later required
- Bandaged state
- Packed state
- Tourniquet state
- Stabilized state

Treatments should affect individual wound instances rather than a generic health
value assigned to an entire body part.


BLEEDING AND BLOOD VOLUME
-------------------------

The player should have a configured blood volume measured in millilitres instead of
a generic hit-point total. A typical configured starting value might be around
5,000 ml, but character configuration should control the exact amount.

The system should separately track:

- Current blood volume
- External blood-loss rate
- Internal blood-loss rate

Bleeding can be categorized as:

- Capillary: slow bleeding
- Venous: moderate continuous bleeding
- Arterial: rapid and immediately dangerous bleeding
- Internal: blood loss that is not externally visible

Blood loss should progressively cause believable consequences:

- Reduced stamina
- Poor weapon stability
- Slower movement
- Tunnel vision
- Confusion
- Reduced consciousness
- Unconsciousness
- Circulatory arrest


PHYSIOLOGICAL SYSTEMS
---------------------

The medical state should be divided into three interacting systems.

CIRCULATION

- Blood volume
- Active bleeding
- Perfusion
- Circulatory arrest

RESPIRATION

- Breathing effectiveness
- Chest trauma
- Airway condition
- Oxygenation
- Respiratory arrest

NEUROLOGICAL CONDITION

- Brain and nervous-system trauma
- Pain response
- Consciousness
- Incapacitation

The initial calculations may be simplified, but they should preserve realistic
causal relationships. For example, severe blood loss should reduce perfusion, and
reduced perfusion should gradually reduce consciousness.


INCAPACITATION AND DEATH
------------------------

Death should result from explicit medical causes rather than health reaching zero.

Possible fatal conditions:

- Catastrophic brain injury
- Destruction of critical cervical nervous tissue
- Unsurvivable torso trauma
- Critical blood loss
- Prolonged lack of oxygen
- Cardiac arrest without successful intervention

Unconsciousness must remain separate from death. An unconscious player may still:

- Continue bleeding
- Breathe inadequately
- Be examined and treated by another player
- Recover if circulation and oxygenation improve
- Die if the underlying cause remains untreated

Ragdoll activation should represent loss of muscular control. It should not itself
mean that the player is medically dead.


TREATMENTS
----------

Treatments should control or manage specific medical causes. They should never
restore a generic health bar.

Recommended initial treatments:

- Direct pressure
- Bandage
- Wound packing
- Tourniquet for limbs
- Chest seal
- Recovery position
- Basic airway management

Possible later treatments:

- Needle decompression
- Blood transfusion
- Intravenous fluids
- Splints
- Analgesics
- Advanced airway intervention
- Cardiopulmonary resuscitation

Important restrictions:

- Bandages do not replace lost blood.
- Tourniquets cannot be applied to the chest, abdomen, neck, or pelvis.
- Painkillers do not repair damaged tissue.
- CPR cannot reverse catastrophic trauma.
- Treatment actions should take time and be interruptible.
- Severe injuries should continue affecting the player after stabilization.


FUNCTIONAL IMPAIRMENT
---------------------

The medical system should expose calculated impairment values instead of directly
editing unrelated subsystems.

Examples of medical queries:

- Movement-speed multiplier
- Stamina multiplier
- Aim-stability multiplier
- Whether sprinting is medically possible
- Whether an arm can support a weapon
- Whether the player is conscious
- Whether the player can perform treatment

Movement, animation, weapon, camera, and IK systems should read these values or react
to medical signals. This keeps medical logic independent and testable.


SIGNALS AND SYSTEM INTEGRATION
------------------------------

Recommended signals:

- injury_added
- injury_updated
- bleeding_changed
- treatment_started
- treatment_interrupted
- treatment_completed
- consciousness_changed
- incapacitated
- circulation_stopped
- respiration_stopped
- medically_died

The MedicalSystem should request final death through BasePlayer. BasePlayer remains
responsible for coordinating input shutdown, camera state, ragdoll activation, and
other player subsystems.

The notification system can react to medical signals and display warnings or
instructions. MedicalSystem should not directly create UI controls.


SIMULATION FREQUENCY
--------------------

Damage application must be immediate. Long-term physiological simulation does not
need to run every rendered frame.

A fixed medical update frequency of approximately 5 to 10 updates per second should
be sufficient for bleeding, circulation, respiration, and consciousness. A fixed
tick makes the simulation cheaper, easier to test, and easier to synchronize in
future multiplayer development.


RECOMMENDED DEVELOPMENT PHASES
------------------------------

PHASE 1 — DAMAGE AND ANATOMY

- Standardized damage context
- Anatomical hit-region mapping
- Persistent wound instances

PHASE 2 — BLEEDING

- External bleeding
- Blood volume
- Blood-loss symptoms
- Exsanguination

PHASE 3 — BASIC TREATMENT

- Direct pressure
- Bandages
- Wound packing
- Tourniquets
- Interruptible treatment actions

PHASE 4 — CONSCIOUSNESS

- Perfusion effects
- Unconsciousness
- Incapacitation
- Separation of incapacitation from death

PHASE 5 — RESPIRATION AND INTERNAL TRAUMA

- Internal bleeding
- Chest wounds
- Oxygenation
- Respiratory arrest

PHASE 6 — FUNCTIONAL INJURIES

- Fractures
- Limb impairment
- Movement penalties
- Weapon-handling and IK consequences

PHASE 7 — COOPERATIVE MEDICINE

- Examination of other players
- Treating other players
- Multiplayer authority and synchronization

PHASE 8 — ADVANCED MEDICINE

- Advanced airway care
- Blood products and fluids
- CPR
- Detailed organ and tissue modelling


CORE DEVELOPMENT RULE
---------------------

MODEL MEDICAL CAUSES AND THEIR CONSEQUENCES. DO NOT IMPLEMENT A TRADITIONAL HEALTH BAR.

Damage should create anatomical trauma. Trauma should create wounds and physiological
effects. Treatment should manage those effects. Incapacitation and death should be
the results of the simulated medical state.
